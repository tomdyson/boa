Build your own little apps

When I build simple web apps, I no longer write code - I describe what I want to an AI coding assistant, which I've already taught how to make apps and host them for free on a platform called Cloudflare.

This guide gets you set up to do the same, on a Mac. By the end you'll have your own app machine, called boa: say "make me a page where friends can pick dates for dinner" and a few minutes later you'll have a private web page, with a password, where people tick boxes and leave notes. Or say "make a sign-up page for my Tuesday classes, twelve places each" and you'll have a booking page anyone can use. Answers and bookings will save as they go, and you can ask Claude what everyone said or who's coming.

This page is itself one of those apps. Open it on the Mac you'll be building on, tick things off as you go, and if you get stuck, write in the box at the end of each step. I'll see what you write, though not straight away: if you want to carry on today, message me as well. Your ticks and notes are private: only you and I can see them. They're tied to this browser, so if you'd like to carry on from another computer, ask me for a link that brings them with you.

How it fits together

Claude does the technical work: it writes the code, runs the commands and fixes its own mistakes. You stay in charge: you say what you want, approve what it does, and check the result. You won't need to type anything into the Terminal yourself.

Cloudflare runs your apps on the internet. Its free plan is far more than you'll need, and you won't need to give it a card.

boa is the app machine itself: a small, free tool I wrote that Claude installs on your Cloudflare account in step 5. It's the same for everyone who follows this guide, and anyone can read how it works at github.com/tomdyson/boa.

Allow about an hour for steps 1 to 6, with a cup of tea. Some of that is waiting while Claude works.

When things go wrong

  • Errors are normal. Claude hits them constantly and usually fixes them itself. If it seems stuck, copy the error (or take a screenshot and drag it in) and say "this happened, what does it mean and what should we do?"
  • Ask it to explain. "Explain that like I've never done this before" always works. So does "why are you asking me that?"
  • If it's going round in circles, press the stop button and say "Stop. Tell me in plain English what you're trying to do and what's going wrong."
  • A long conversation gets muddled. Start a fresh one and say "Run boa doctor and tell me in plain English what it says."
  • If in doubt about a permission request, say no and ask what it was for. Apart from your Apps folder, Claude should only need four places, all in your home folder: where it installed Node.js, its own skills (.claude), boa's settings (.config) and the boa command (.local, plus one line in a file called .zshrc so your Mac can find it).
  • Or write in the box under the step you're on and I'll have a look.
